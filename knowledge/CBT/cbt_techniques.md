# Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) Techniques & Clinical Principles

## Core CBT Principles
Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) operates on the principle that our thoughts, feelings, physical sensations, and actions are interconnected. Negative and unhelpful thoughts can trap individuals in vicious cycles. CBT aims to help people break these cycles by breaking down problems into smaller parts and reframing cognitive distortions.

## Common Cognitive Distortions
1. **Catastrophizing**: Expecting the absolute worst-case scenario to happen without objective evidence.
2. **Black-and-White Thinking**: Viewing situations in binary all-or-nothing terms (e.g., "If I am not perfect, I am a failure").
3. **Emotional Reasoning**: Assuming that because you feel a certain way, it must reflect reality (e.g., "I feel terrified, so I must be in grave danger").
4. **Mind Reading**: Assuming you know what others are thinking without checking.
5. **Overgeneralization**: Drawing broad conclusions from a single event.

## Key CBT Interventions
### 1. Thought Decoupling & Cognitive Restructuring
- **Identify**: Notice automatic negative thoughts (ANTs) as they arise.
- **Examine Evidence**: Ask: *What is the objective evidence supporting this thought? What is the evidence against it?*
- **Alternative Perspective**: Reframe the thought to a balanced, compassionate, and realistic alternative.

### 2. Behavioral Activation
- Schedule small, manageable daily activities that provide a sense of achievement, connection, or pleasure.
- Break overwhelming goals into micro-steps (2-minute rule) to overcome behavioral paralysis in depression and burnout.

### 3. Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP)
- Gradual, controlled exposure to anxiety-provoking situations without engaging in avoidance or safety behaviors, allowing habituation to occur.
